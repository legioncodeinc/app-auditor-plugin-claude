## Headline figure and cited authority

According to the article, the **National Center for Voice and Speech** established that "the average conversation rate for English speakers in the United States is about 150 wpm."

## Speaking rate ranges by context (as reported on the page)

| Context | Words per minute (WPM) |
|---|---|
| Presentations | 100-150 wpm |
| Conversational speech | 120-150 wpm |
| Audiobook narration | 150-160 wpm (upper limit for comfortable comprehension) |
| Radio hosts / podcasters | 150-160 wpm |
| Auctioneers | ~250 wpm |
| Commentators | 250-400 wpm |

The article also notes that among five analyzed TED Talks, the average speaking rate was 173 wpm, with individual talks ranging from 154 to 201 wpm - suggesting professional/prepared speakers often exceed the conversational baseline.

## Cross-referenced figures from other sources found during research (not separately archived, noted for corroboration)

- General conversational English: 125-150 wpm (multiple sources).
- Standard narration pace: ~150-160 wpm.
- Professional audiobook narration: ~130-160 wpm.
- Commercials (fast, time-constrained delivery): ~180-200 wpm.
- Documentary/educational narration (slower, allows comprehension time): ~150-170 wpm.
- Information-dense technical voiceover: recommended as low as ~75-90 wpm for maximum comprehension.

## Practical takeaway for a narration-script generator

A safe default assumption for product-demo narration (a semi-prepared, moderately-paced style, not conversational and not auctioneer-fast) is **130-160 words per minute**, consistent with both this source's "presentations"/"audiobook narration" bands and the demo-script-structure source's assumed 130-150 wpm pace (see `demo--demo-script-structure.md` in this archive).
