## What it is

An interface inventory represents "a comprehensive collection of the bits and pieces that make up your interface." Unlike content inventories that catalog textual material, this approach systematically documents and organizes all UI components, buttons, headers, forms, carousels, and other interface elements, across a digital product.

## Conducting an interface inventory

The process involves five key steps:

1. **Preparation**: ready your screenshot tools and access the project.
2. **Template setup**: use presentation software like Keynote or PowerPoint to establish organizational categories.
3. **Capture**: screenshot distinct component treatments, not every instance, but variations in styling (e.g. "a button with a bevel and right-facing caret vs another without").
4. **Categorization**: group similar treatments together for side-by-side comparison.
5. **Presentation**: share findings with stakeholders.

## Key benefits

- **Foundation for design systems**: serves as "the first step at deconstructing pages down to their atomic level."
- **Organizational alignment**: establishes common interface language across teams and agencies.
- **Consistency exposure**: reveals unintentional redundancy and design inconsistencies.
- **Scope management**: ensures no components are overlooked during redesigns.
- **Responsive analysis**: helps teams assess component difficulty for responsive adaptation.

## Real-world example

Frost contrasts PNC Bank's inconsistent button variations with Etsy's streamlined approach, demonstrating how deliberate design systems prevent unintentional inconsistencies.
